Protecting the Earth for tomorrow

Our Goal

Help people understand and reduce the environmental, social, and personal costs of the way they use AI, so they keep the benefits and shrink the downsides.

Why it Matters

AI has become part of everyday life faster than almost any technology before it. People now use it to write, search, learn, create, and make decisions, often many times a day. But the costs of that use are mostly invisible, and costs that can't be seen don't get managed.

If you want to learn more about the impacts check it out here: Impacts

  • The footprint is real, and growing. Every AI response runs on data centers that draw electricity and water. One prompt is small, but billions of them a day are not, and demand is rising faster than most people realize. Unlike a car or a light bill, AI gives users no signal of what they're consuming.
  • The effects reach other people. AI output shapes what people read, believe, and trust. Careless use spreads errors and bias, floods shared spaces with unlabeled content, and draws on the work of writers, artists, and others who rarely get a say. One person's choices add up to a shared problem.
  • The user pays too. Leaning on AI for everything can dull the skills and judgment people worked to build. Sensitive information shared in a chat can be hard to take back. And convenience can quietly replace thinking where thinking matters most.
  • Habits are forming now. How people use AI in these early years will set the norms for decades. It is far easier to build good habits at the start than to undo bad ones once they're settled.
  • Nobody is helping the individual. Most responsible AI work focuses on the companies building the models. Very little helps the ordinary person or team using them. That gap is where we work.

The bottom line: AI's benefits are worth keeping, and the choice isn't between using AI and not using it. It's between using it blindly and using it well. We exist to make using it well possible for everyone.
