For Individuals

AI Energy Score Leaderboard

Compares how much energy different AI models use for the same task, so you can pick a smaller, more efficient one.

IEA: Key Questions on Energy and AI

The International Energy Agency's plain-language overview of how much electricity AI uses and where demand is heading.

Oxford Offsetting Principles

University of Oxford guidance on choosing credible carbon offsets and avoiding low-quality ones.

e-Stewards

Find a certified recycler for old phones, laptops, and other electronics.


For Businesses

Software Carbon Intensity for AI (Green Software Foundation)

A standard method for measuring the carbon emissions of AI systems, with a free introductory course.

CodeCarbon

A free, open-source tool that tracks the energy and emissions of AI running on your own hardware. For AI used through outside services, its documentation points to a companion tool called EcoLogits.

AI Energy Score Leaderboard

Energy ratings for AI models, useful when choosing vendors and matching models to tasks.

Oxford Offsetting Principles

Guidance for building a credible offsetting strategy that avoids greenwashing.

EPA: Certified Electronics Recyclers

How to find recyclers certified to the R2 or e-Stewards standards for retired equipment.

UN report: Environmental Cost of AI's Energy Use

Summary of the 2026 United Nations University report on AI's carbon, water, and land footprints, useful for sustainability reporting.


For Schools

UNESCO: Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research

UNESCO's global guidance for schools on setting AI policies, protecting student data, and keeping learning human-centred.

Pacific Institute: How Much Water Do Data Centers Use?

A short question-and-answer brief that works well as a classroom reading on AI and water.

AI Energy Score Leaderboard

Real data students can explore in lessons or projects comparing the energy use of AI models.

CodeCarbon

Lets computer science students measure the emissions of their own code, a good fit for student-led projects.

EPA: Certified Electronics Recyclers

Guidance on recycling school devices responsibly at the end of their life.
