The Impacts That AI are Having


Negative Effects
  • Electricity Usage.
  • Carbon Emissions.
  • Water Usage.
  • Electronic waste.
  • Efficiency isn’t a full fix.

AI’s environmental impact comes from the data centers that power it, and it is growing quickly. The International Energy Agency’s 2026 figures show data center electricity use at 485 TWh in 2025 and heading toward roughly 950 TWh by 2030, close to 3% of global demand, with AI-focused facilities up 50% in a single year. That power carries a carbon cost: a United Nations University report estimates AI-related emissions could reach 400 million tonnes of CO2-equivalent, similar to the UK’s total for 2025. Water is the most debated part of the picture. Data centers use it for cooling, but most of AI’s water footprint sits at the power plants that supply them, with direct cooling making up only about 4%. Nationally the share is small, yet the Pacific Institute notes that the strain is felt locally and peaks in the hottest months. adds a further cost, since the UN report projects up to 2.5 million metric tons of AI-related e-waste a year by 2030, much of it likely to end up in poorer countries. And although each AI task is becoming more efficient, the same report warns that lower costs drive more use, which can wipe out the savings

AI is still a small share of the world’s energy and water use, and it can help solve environmental problems as well as cause them. What makes it a concern is how fast its footprint is growing and how unevenly the costs fall on particular communities and countries. Because efficiency gains alone won’t offset rising demand, how people choose to use AI matters. Knowing these costs is the first step toward reducing them.
